RESEARCH / ROBOTIC AUTOMATION

Multi-robot battery disassembly.

Planning the order and robot assignment of screw-removal tasks on a simulated electric vehicle battery.

03 / 06
Institution
IIT Madras
Timeline
January – May 2025
Tools
Fusion 360 · RoboDK · genetic algorithm
Model
112 screws · 4, 5, and 6 robot setups

Overview

I modeled a simplified EV battery inspired by a Chevrolet Bolt battery, with 112 screws, eight modules, and eight busbars. The model was built in Fusion 360 and transferred to RoboDK to plan UR10e robot motions for screw removal.

The scheduling problem was to assign each screw to a reachable robot and order tasks so that the robots could work in parallel without violating access or collision constraints.

01 / METHOD

Optimizing a constrained sequence

  • Created approach and unfastening targets in RoboDK and collected robot–screw travel times.
  • Recorded collision constraints between robot paths and a hard precedence rule: the 60 exterior screws must be removed before the 52 interior screws.
  • Represented each candidate sequence as robot–screw pairs in a genetic algorithm, scoring it with cycle time plus 0.25 times cumulative idle time.
02 / RESULT

Five robots balanced the workload

The study compared four-, five-, and six-robot workstations. Three robots could not cover the full battery workspace. Among the tested configurations, five robots gave the best balance: four increased individual workloads and cycle time, while six crowded the workspace and sharply increased idle time.

The result applies to this simplified battery model and simulated screw-removal task. It does not represent an automated end-to-end battery disassembly line.
